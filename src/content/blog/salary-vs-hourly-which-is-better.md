When hiring new employees, one of the first decisions you'll face is whether to offer a salary or an hourly wage. Both models have distinct advantages and drawbacks depending on the role, the industry, and your business goals.

## The Case for Hourly Pay

Hourly employees are paid for the exact amount of time they work. This model is common in retail, hospitality, and manufacturing.

*   **Pros:** Flexibility for both parties; pay matches productivity; clear overtime eligibility.
*   **Cons:** Administrative burden of tracking hours; variable costs for the employer.

## The Case for Salaried Pay

Salaried employees receive a fixed amount of pay per period, regardless of how many hours they work (subject to exemption rules). This is standard for professional and managerial roles.

*   **Pros:** Budget predictability; less administrative overhead; perceived higher status.
*   **Cons:** Potential for burnout if hours are excessive; risk of misclassification (exempt vs. non-exempt).

## Key Considerations

When choosing, consider the nature of the work. If the role requires fixed hours and physical presence, hourly pay is often simpler. For creative or management roles where results matter more than "time in seat," a salary might be more appropriate.

## Conclusion

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. Successful businesses often use a mix of both models tailored to specific roles. Understanding the legal and financial implications of each is the first step toward building a sustainable workforce.

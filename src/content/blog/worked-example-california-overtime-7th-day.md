California has the strictest overtime rules in the United States. This walkthrough shows how to calculate a complex California paycheck with daily overtime, 7th-consecutive-day rules, and double-time using the [overtime calculator](/calculators/overtime-calculator).

## The Scenario

Carlos works at a California warehouse earning **$22.00/hour**. During a busy week, he worked the following schedule:

- **Monday–Friday**: 10 hours each day (50 hours total)
- **Saturday**: 8 hours
- **Sunday**: 10 hours (7th consecutive day)

Under California labor law (Labor Code § 510), this triggers multiple overtime rules simultaneously.

## Step 1: Identify California Overtime Rules

California has FOUR overtime tiers:

1. **Daily overtime**: Hours over 8 in a single day = 1.5x
2. **Daily double-time**: Hours over 12 in a single day = 2.0x
3. **Weekly overtime**: Hours over 40 in a workweek = 1.5x (but only the hours NOT already paid at double-time)
4. **7th consecutive day**: First 8 hours = 1.5x, hours over 8 = 2.0x

## Step 2: Calculate Each Day's Pay

### Monday through Friday (10 hours each)

For each weekday:
- Hours 1-8 (regular): 8 × $22.00 = $176.00
- Hours 9-10 (daily OT at 1.5x): 2 × $33.00 = $66.00
- Total per weekday: $176.00 + $66.00 = **$242.00**
- Total for 5 weekdays: $242.00 × 5 = **$1,210.00**

### Saturday (8 hours, 6th consecutive day)

Saturday is the 6th consecutive day but does NOT trigger 7th-day rules. However, weekly overtime has already kicked in:
- Total hours so far this week: 50 + 8 = 58 hours
- All 8 Saturday hours are over 40, so they're paid at 1.5x
- 8 × $33.00 = **$264.00**

### Sunday (10 hours, 7th consecutive day)

Sunday triggers the 7th-consecutive-day rule:
- First 8 hours on 7th day: 1.5x → 8 × $33.00 = $264.00
- Hours 9-10 on 7th day: 2.0x (double time) → 2 × $44.00 = $88.00
- Total for Sunday: $264.00 + $88.00 = **$352.00**

## Step 3: Calculate Total Gross Pay

> Mon-Fri regular + OT: $1,210.00
> Saturday (all OT): $264.00
> Sunday (7th day OT + DT): $352.00
> **Total weekly gross: $1,826.00**

## Step 4: Compare to Federal FLSA Calculation

Under federal FLSA alone (40-hour threshold):
- Total hours: 60
- Regular hours: 40 × $22.00 = $880.00
- Overtime hours (20): 20 × $33.00 = $660.00
- Total: **$1,540.00**

Carlos earns **$286.00 MORE per week** under California rules vs. federal FLSA — that's $14,872/year more if he works this schedule consistently.

## Step 5: Calculate Annualized Pay

If Carlos works this schedule 50 weeks per year (2 weeks vacation):

> $1,826.00 × 50 = **$91,300 annual gross pay**

Despite the "low" $22/hour base rate, California's strict overtime rules mean Carlos earns over $91K annually.

## Step 6: Verify With the Calculator

Enter Carlos's numbers into the [overtime calculator](/calculators/overtime-calculator):
- Hourly rate: $22.00
- Regular hours: 40 (federal portion)
- Overtime hours: 20 (California daily + 7th day)
- Double-time hours: 2 (Sunday hours 9-10)

You should see approximately **$1,826 weekly gross**.

## Common Mistakes in California Overtime

1. **Forgetting daily overtime** — many employers only count weekly overtime (40-hour threshold) and miss the 8-hour daily rule
2. **Not tracking consecutive days** — the 7th-day rule applies regardless of total hours
3. **Confusing double-time triggers** — double-time is only after 12 hours in a day OR after 8 hours on the 7th day
4. **Miscalculating regular rate** — bonuses and shift differentials must be included in the "regular rate" used for the 1.5x multiplier

## Key Takeaways

- California daily overtime (over 8 hours/day) is the **most employee-friendly rule** in the US
- The 7th-consecutive-day rule adds significant pay for workers who don't get a rest day
- Multi-rule weeks can produce pay 18%+ higher than federal FLSA alone
- Always track hours per day AND consecutive days worked in California

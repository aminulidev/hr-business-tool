Every paycheck distributed to an American employee represents a calculation governed by federal, state, and local tax statutes. For business owners, payroll administrators, and workers alike, understanding the difference between **mandatory (statutory) deductions** and **voluntary deductions** is essential for maintaining tax compliance and preventing costly penalties.

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS), the Social Security Administration (SSA), and state departments of revenue enforce strict withholding timetables. 

This guide breaks down every line item on a standard pay stub, the statutory formulas applied, and the order in which deductions must be taken.

---

## 1. What Are Statutory Payroll Deductions?

Statutory payroll deductions are withholdings required by law. Employers have a legal obligation to withhold these funds from employee wages and remit them directly to the appropriate governmental tax authorities.

## 2. Breakdown of Mandatory Federal Withholdings

### A. Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA)
FICA taxes fund the federal Social Security and Medicare entitlement programs. Both the employee and the employer pay into FICA:

| Tax Component | Employee Rate | Employer Rate | Combined Rate | Wage Limit (2026 Statutory) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Social Security (OASDI)** | 6.2% | 6.2% | 12.4% | Applies up to statutory wage base |
| **Medicare (HI)** | 1.45% | 1.45% | 2.90% | No wage cap (applies to all wages) |
| **Additional Medicare Tax** | 0.90% | 0.00% | 0.90% | Wages exceeding $200,000 (single) / $250,000 (joint) |

> **Self-Employed Note:** Independent contractors and sole proprietors pay both portions (15.3% combined) via the **Self-Employment Tax (SECA)**.

### B. Federal Income Tax Withholding (FIT)
Federal income tax is calculated using the employee's Form W-4 and the tax tables in **IRS Publication 15-T**. FIT is progressive, meaning higher portions of taxable income are subject to higher marginal rates (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, 37%).

## 3. Employer-Only Payroll Taxes (Not Deducted from Wages)

Employees frequently confuse employer payroll taxes with their personal withholdings. The following taxes are paid **entirely by the business** and are never deducted from employee paychecks:

1. **Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA):** 6.0% on the first $7,000 of employee wages, usually offset by a maximum credit of 5.4% for state unemployment taxes, resulting in an effective rate of **0.6% ($42 per employee annually)**.
2. **State Unemployment Tax Act (SUTA):** Varies by state and employer experience rating, funding state unemployment benefit reserves.
3. **Employer FICA Match:** The business matches the 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare contributions dollar-for-dollar.

## 4. Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Deductions: The Math Matters

The sequence of deductions significantly impacts an employee's tax liability:

* **Pre-Tax Deductions:** Subtracted from gross wages **before** calculating income and/or FICA taxes. Examples include traditional 401(k) contributions, Section 125 health insurance premiums, and Health Savings Accounts (HSA).
  * *Benefit:* Reduces taxable income, lowering FIT and state tax withholdings.
* **Post-Tax Deductions:** Subtracted **after** all taxes have been computed. Examples include Roth 401(k) contributions, wage garnishments, child support orders, and charitable donations.

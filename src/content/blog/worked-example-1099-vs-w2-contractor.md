Should you take a $90/hour 1099 contractor role or a $75/hour W-2 employee role for the same work? This walkthrough uses the [after-tax income calculator](/calculators/after-tax-income-calculator) and [FICA tax calculator](/calculators/fica-tax-calculator) to compare true take-home.

## The Scenario

Jordan has two offers for the same software engineering work:

- **Option A: 1099 Contractor** at **$90/hour**, 40 hours/week, 50 weeks/year (2 weeks unpaid)
- **Option B: W-2 Employee** at **$75/hour**, 40 hours/week, 52 weeks/year (paid vacation)

Let's calculate the true annual take-home for each.

## Step 1: Calculate Gross Annual Income

### Option A (1099 Contractor)

> $90/hour × 40 hours × 50 weeks = **$180,000 gross**

### Option B (W-2 Employee)

> $75/hour × 40 hours × 52 weeks = **$156,000 gross**

The 1099 option pays $24,000 MORE in gross — but contractors pay both halves of FICA.

## Step 2: Calculate Self-Employment Tax (SECA) for 1099

Contractors pay the full 15.3% SECA tax (both employer + employee portions of FICA):

- Social Security: 12.4% on first $176,100 (2026 wage base)
- Medicare: 2.9% on all wages
- Additional Medicare: 0.9% on wages over $200,000 (single)

For $180,000 net SECA income:

> Social Security: $176,100 × 12.4% = $21,836.40
> Medicare: $180,000 × 2.9% = $5,220.00
> **Total SECA tax: $27,056.40**

But there's a deduction: contractors can deduct the employer half (7.65%) from gross income before calculating income tax:

> SECA deduction: $180,000 × 7.65% / 2 = $6,885.00
> Adjusted gross for income tax: $180,000 − $6,885 = **$173,115**

## Step 3: Calculate FICA for W-2 Employee

Employees pay only the employee half of FICA (7.65%):

> Social Security: $176,100 × 6.2% = $10,918.20 (capped at wage base)
> Medicare: $156,000 × 1.45% = $2,262.00
> **Total FICA: $13,180.20**

Employer pays the other half ($13,180.20) — invisible to the employee but a real cost to the employer.

## Step 4: Calculate Federal Income Tax

For 2026 (single filer, $15,300 standard deduction):

### Option A (1099, AGI $173,115)

> Taxable income: $173,115 − $15,300 = $157,815

Bracket calculation:
- 10% on first $12,150: $1,215
- 12% on $12,150-$49,350 ($37,200): $4,464
- 22% on $49,350-$105,250 ($55,900): $12,298
- 24% on $105,250-$157,815 ($52,565): $12,615.60
- **Total federal income tax: $30,592.60**

### Option B (W-2, AGI $156,000)

> Taxable income: $156,000 − $15,300 = $140,700

Bracket calculation:
- 10% on first $12,150: $1,215
- 12% on $12,150-$49,350 ($37,200): $4,464
- 22% on $49,350-$105,250 ($55,900): $12,298
- 24% on $105,250-$140,700 ($35,450): $8,508
- **Total federal income tax: $26,485**

## Step 5: Calculate Total Tax Burden

| Tax | Option A (1099) | Option B (W-2) |
|---|---|---|
| Federal income tax | $30,592.60 | $26,485.00 |
| SECA tax (1099 only) | $27,056.40 | — |
| FICA tax (W-2 only) | — | $13,180.20 |
| State tax (5% average) | $8,655.75 | $7,800.00 |
| **Total tax** | **$66,304.75** | **$47,465.20** |
| **Effective tax rate** | **36.8%** | **30.4%** |

## Step 6: Calculate Net Take-Home

### Option A (1099)

> Gross: $180,000
> Total tax: −$66,304.75
> **Net take-home: $113,695.25**

### Option B (W-2)

> Gross: $156,000
> Total tax: −$47,465.20
> **Net take-home: $108,534.80**

**Option A pays $5,160.45 more in net take-home** — about 4.8% more.

## Step 7: Factor in Benefits (The Hidden Cost)

But the comparison isn't complete. W-2 employees typically receive benefits contractors don't:

| Benefit | Annual Value |
|---|---|
| Health insurance (employer contribution) | $12,000 |
| 401(k) match (3% of $156k) | $4,680 |
| Paid time off (already included in 52-week pay) | $0 (counted above) |
| Other benefits (life, dental, vision) | $1,500 |
| **Total benefits** | **$18,180** |

Adjusted take-home (including benefits value):

### Option A (1099) — must buy own benefits

> Net take-home: $113,695.25
> Minus health insurance: −$12,000 (individual plan, not employer-subsidized)
> Minus 401(k) match foregone: $0 (can still contribute, just no match)
> **Effective take-home: $101,695.25**

### Option B (W-2) — benefits included

> Net take-home: $108,534.80
> Plus benefits value: +$18,180 (already net of personal cost)
> **Effective total compensation: $126,714.80**

When benefits are included, **Option B (W-2) is worth $25,019.55 more** than Option A.

## Step 8: Calculate Effective Hourly Rate

### Option A (1099)

> Effective compensation: $101,695.25
> Hours worked: 2,000 (40 × 50 weeks)
> **Effective hourly rate: $50.85/hour**

The $90 nominal rate drops to **$50.85 effective** after taxes and self-purchased benefits — a 43.5% reduction.

### Option B (W-2)

> Effective compensation: $126,714.80
> Hours worked: 2,080 (40 × 52 weeks)
> **Effective hourly rate: $60.92/hour**

The $75 nominal rate drops to **$60.92 effective** — only an 18.8% reduction, because benefits cover much of the gap.

## Step 10: When 1099 Makes Sense

The 1099 option wins when:

1. **You have legitimate business expenses** to deduct (home office, equipment, travel)
2. **You can charge significantly more per hour** (2x+ the W-2 rate)
3. **You don't need benefits** (covered by spouse's employer)
4. **You want schedule flexibility** (1099 typically has more autonomy)
5. **You can contribute to a Solo 401(k)** with higher limits ($69,000 in 2026 vs $23,000 for W-2 401k)

## Step 11: When W-2 Makes Sense

The W-2 option wins when:

1. **Benefits matter** (health insurance for family, 401k match, paid leave)
2. **You want predictable income** (no gaps between contracts)
3. **You want unemployment insurance** (1099 contractors can't collect UI)
4. **You want workers comp coverage** (1099 must self-insure)
5. **The hourly rate gap is small** (under 30%)

## Common Mistakes in 1099 vs W-2 Comparison

1. **Forgetting SECA tax** — 1099 contractors pay both halves of FICA (15.3% vs 7.65%)
2. **Ignoring benefits value** — health insurance alone can be worth $12k+/year
3. **Not accounting for unpaid time** — 1099 contractors often have 2-4 weeks unpaid between gigs
4. **Forgetting Solo 401(k) advantage** — 1099 can shelter much more income from tax
5. **Not tracking business expenses** — 1099s can deduct home office, equipment, mileage

## Key Takeaways

- **Gross rate comparison is misleading** — always calculate effective take-home including benefits
- **The 1099 premium must be 30%+ to break even** with W-2 after taxes and benefits
- **SECA tax (15.3%) is the biggest 1099 cost** — $13,876/year more than FICA at $180k income
- **Benefits can be worth 10-15% of salary** — health insurance, 401k match, paid leave

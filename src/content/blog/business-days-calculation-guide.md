Whether you are managing employee payroll direct deposits, negotiating commercial contract milestones, meeting financial clearing requirements (T+1 securities settlement), or satisfying HR grievance filing deadlines, understanding how to calculate **business days** is crucial.

Confusing "calendar days" with "business days" or misinterpreting statutory holiday schedules causes missed legal deadlines, late ACH processing penalties, and breach-of-contract disputes.

This guide explains the rules for business day calculation, official US federal holiday conventions, banking day cutoffs, and practical project scheduling methods.

---

## 1. Calendar Days vs. Business Days vs. Banking Days

Contracts and corporate policies often use different definitions of time:

* **Calendar Days:** Every consecutive day on the calendar, including Saturdays, Sundays, and public holidays. A 30-day notice period in a commercial lease usually means 30 continuous calendar days.
* **Business Days (Working Days):** Monday through Friday, excluding defined public or statutory holidays. Weekends are always excluded.
* **Banking Days (Fedwire Days):** Days when the Federal Reserve Bank and commercial banks are officially open for business. If a holiday falls on Sunday, banks observe it on Monday; transactions do not clear until the following Tuesday.

| Period Type | What It Includes | Common Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Calendar Days** | All 7 days of the week | Residential lease notices, warranty expiration, statutory cooling-off periods |
| **Business Days** | Mon–Fri (excluding federal holidays) | Vendor SLAs, contract milestones, HR investigation timelines, shipping estimates |
| **Banking Days** | Federal Reserve open dates | ACH direct deposit clearing, wire transfers, check hold clearance |

## 2. Official US Federal Holidays (Title 5 U.S.C. § 6103)

Under federal law, the United States recognizes **11 permanent annual federal holidays**:

1. **New Year’s Day:** January 1
2. **Martin Luther King Jr.’s Birthday:** Third Monday in January
3. **Washington’s Birthday (Presidents' Day):** Third Monday in February
4. **Memorial Day:** Last Monday in May
5. **Juneteenth National Independence Day:** June 19
6. **Independence Day:** July 4
7. **Labor Day:** First Monday in September
8. **Columbus Day:** Second Monday in October
9. **Veterans Day:** November 11
10. **Thanksgiving Day:** Fourth Thursday in November
11. **Christmas Day:** December 25

### The In-Lieu-Of Holiday Rule:
* If a federal holiday falls on a **Saturday**, the preceding **Friday** is treated as an observed federal holiday.
* If a holiday falls on a **Sunday**, the following **Monday** is treated as an observed federal holiday.

## 3. Payroll Timing: The "2-Day Rule" for ACH Direct Deposits

Under National Automated Clearing House Association (NACHA) guidelines, standard direct deposit payroll requires transmitting the ACH payroll file **at least two banking days prior to payday**.

### Practical Payroll Scenario:
* **Target Employee Payday:** Friday, July 3 (because July 4 is Independence Day).
* **Holiday Observation:** If July 4 is Saturday, July 3 is the observed banking holiday.
* **Impact:** Banks are closed Friday, July 3. Payday must shift to **Thursday, July 2**.
* **ACH Transmission Deadline:** To fund accounts by Thursday morning, payroll must be transmitted on **Tuesday, June 30** before banking cutoff (typically 5:00 PM EST).

Missing this holiday offset causes employee paychecks to clear on Monday, July 6, potentially violating state prompt-payment statutes.

Sales compensation plans can be complex — base salary, tiered commission rates, quota accelerators, and split deals all combine to determine a sales rep's total earnings. This walkthrough uses the [sales commission calculator](/calculators/commission-calculator) to break down a real-world scenario.

## The Scenario

Alex is a SaaS account executive with the following compensation plan:

- **Base salary**: $50,000/year
- **Quota**: $100,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) per quarter
- **Commission structure**:
  - 0-50% to quota: 3% commission
  - 50-100% to quota: 5% commission
  - 100-150% to quota: 8% commission (accelerator)
  - Above 150% to quota: 12% commission (super-accelerator)
- **Quarterly sales**: $145,000 (145% to quota)

Let's calculate Alex's quarterly commission.

## Step 1: Calculate the Base Salary Per Quarter

> $50,000 / 4 quarters = **$12,500 base per quarter**

## Step 2: Calculate Commission in Each Tier

The tiered commission means different portions of the $145,000 in sales earn different rates:

### Tier 1: 0-50% of quota ($0 to $50,000)

> $50,000 × 3% = **$1,500 commission**

### Tier 2: 50-100% of quota ($50,000 to $100,000)

> $50,000 × 5% = **$2,500 commission**

### Tier 3: 100-150% of quota ($100,000 to $150,000)

Alex reached $145,000, so this tier is $45,000 (not the full $50,000):

> $45,000 × 8% = **$3,600 commission**

### Tier 4: Above 150% of quota (above $150,000)

Alex did not reach this tier (he was $5,000 short):

> $0 × 12% = **$0 commission**

## Step 3: Calculate Total Quarterly Commission

> $1,500 (Tier 1) + $2,500 (Tier 2) + $3,600 (Tier 3) + $0 (Tier 4) = **$7,600 quarterly commission**

## Step 4: Calculate Total Quarterly Earnings

> Base salary: $12,500
> Commission: $7,600
> **Total quarterly earnings: $20,100**

## Step 5: Calculate Annual Earnings (If Performance Holds)

If Alex maintains this performance all year:

> Base: $50,000
> Commission: $7,600 × 4 = $30,400
> **Total annual earnings: $80,400**

That's **61% more** than his base salary alone — the accelerator tiers really pay off when reps exceed quota.

## Step 6: Calculate Commission Rate (Blended)

To compare this plan to a flat-rate plan, calculate the blended commission rate:

> Total commission / Total sales = $7,600 / $145,000 = **5.24% blended rate**

If Alex had a flat 5% commission on all sales with no base:

> $145,000 × 5% = $7,250 commission (vs $7,600 with tiers)

The tiered structure paid him **$350 more** this quarter because of the accelerator.

## Step 7: What If Alex Hit 200% to Quota?

To see the power of accelerators, let's project if Alex had sold $200,000 (200% to quota):

- Tier 1 ($0-$50k): $50,000 × 3% = $1,500
- Tier 2 ($50k-$100k): $50,000 × 5% = $2,500
- Tier 3 ($100k-$150k): $50,000 × 8% = $4,000
- Tier 4 ($150k-$200k): $50,000 × 12% = $6,000
- Total: **$14,000 commission**

Plus base $12,500 = **$26,500 quarterly earnings**.

Annualized at this performance: $50k base + $56k commission = **$106,000 total earnings**.

You should see approximately **$7,600 commission**.

## Common Mistakes in Commission Plans

1. **Forgetting tier boundaries** — each tier only applies to the dollars within that range, not the total
2. **Miscounting quota attainment** — track against the quarterly quota, not the annual one (most plans reset quarterly)
3. **Ignoring draw vs. commission** — if you have a recoverable draw, you owe back commission if you underperform
4. **Not accounting for splits** — if you split a deal with another rep, you each get 50% credit
5. **Missing accelerator thresholds** — even $1 over a tier boundary can mean thousands in additional commission

## Key Takeaways

- Tiered commission plans with accelerators **massively reward over-quota performance**
- The blended commission rate is the right metric for comparing plans across employers
- Always model your "what-if" scenarios (50%, 100%, 150%, 200% to quota) before accepting a job offer
- Base salary provides downside protection; commission provides upside

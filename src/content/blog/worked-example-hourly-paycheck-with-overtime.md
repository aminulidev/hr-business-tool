If you've ever stared at your pay stub trying to figure out why your take-home is what it is, this walkthrough is for you. We'll calculate a real hourly paycheck step-by-step using the [hourly paycheck calculator](/calculators/hourly-paycheck-calculator), showing every deduction along the way.

## The Scenario

Maria earns **$18.00 per hour** at a warehouse. Last week she worked **45 hours** — 40 regular hours plus 5 overtime hours. She's paid bi-weekly (every two weeks). Her employer withholds **12% federal tax**, **5% state tax**, and the standard **FICA (7.65%)** applies. She has no additional deductions.

## Step 1: Calculate the Regular Pay

For 40 regular hours per week at $18.00/hour, Maria's weekly regular pay is:

> 40 hours × $18.00 = **$720.00/week regular pay**

Over a bi-weekly pay period (2 weeks), that's:

> $720.00 × 2 weeks = **$1,440.00 regular pay per paycheck**

## Step 2: Calculate the Overtime Pay

Under federal FLSA, overtime is paid at **1.5x the regular rate** for hours over 40 in a single workweek. Maria's overtime rate is:

> $18.00 × 1.5 = **$27.00/hour**

For 5 overtime hours per week, her weekly overtime pay is:

> 5 hours × $27.00 = **$135.00/week overtime pay**

Over a bi-weekly pay period:

> $135.00 × 2 weeks = **$270.00 overtime pay per paycheck**

## Step 3: Calculate Gross Pay Per Paycheck

Add regular + overtime:

> $1,440.00 + $270.00 = **$1,710.00 gross pay per bi-weekly paycheck**

## Step 4: Calculate Annual Gross Pay

A bi-weekly pay period means 26 paychecks per year:

> $1,710.00 × 26 = **$44,460.00 annual gross pay**

## Step 5: Calculate Each Deduction

Each deduction is calculated as a percentage of the bi-weekly gross ($1,710.00):

| Deduction | Rate | Amount Per Paycheck |
|---|---|---|
| Federal tax | 12% | $205.20 |
| State tax | 5% | $85.50 |
| FICA (Social Security + Medicare) | 7.65% | $130.82 |
| **Total deductions** | **24.65%** | **$421.52** |

## Step 6: Calculate Net Take-Home Pay

> $1,710.00 (gross) − $421.52 (deductions) = **$1,288.48 net take-home per paycheck**

Annualized:

> $1,288.48 × 26 = **$33,500.48 annual net take-home pay**

## Step 7: Calculate the Effective Hourly Rate

Maria's effective hourly rate (what she actually keeps per hour worked) is:

> $33,500.48 / (45 hours × 52 weeks) = $33,500.48 / 2,340 = **$14.32/hour effective take-home**

Even though her nominal rate is $18.00/hour, after taxes and deductions she keeps **$14.32 per hour worked** — about 79.6% of her nominal rate.

## Key Takeaways

- Overtime at 1.5x adds significant gross income — $135/week becomes $7,020/year in this scenario
- Taxes consume about 24.65% of gross pay at Maria's income level
- The effective hourly rate ($14.32) is the number that matters for budgeting, not the nominal rate ($18.00)
- The marginal tax rate (what Maria pays on her next dollar of income) is 12% federal + 5% state + 7.65% FICA = 24.65%

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Forgetting FICA** — many workers underestimate their deductions by forgetting Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) total 7.65%
2. **Using gross for budgeting** — always budget with net take-home, not gross pay
3. **Miscounting pay periods** — bi-weekly is 26 paychecks/year (not 24, which is semi-monthly)
4. **Ignoring overtime in annual calculations** — if you regularly work OT, include it in your annual gross projection

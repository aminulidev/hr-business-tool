Severance pay is compensation granted to an employee upon involuntary separation from employment, such as during corporate downsizings, reorganizations, reductions in force (RIF), or layoffs.

While federal law under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) does not mandate severance pay, offering severance is standard corporate practice. In exchange for financial severance, employers typically require the departing worker to sign a binding **General Release and Waiver of Claims Agreement**, protecting the company from employment litigation.

This guide explains standard severance calculation formulas, tenure multipliers, supplemental tax rules, and healthcare continuation requirements.

---

## 1. Common Severance Pay Formulas

Severance calculations are typically structured around the employee's **length of service (tenure)** and **organizational seniority**:

```
Severance Payout = Weekly Base Salary × Weeks of Pay per Year of Service × Total Years Completed
```

### Typical Corporate Severance Benchmarks:
| Employee Level | Typical Multiplier per Year of Service | Typical Minimum Floor | Typical Maximum Cap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Non-Exempt / Hourly Staff** | 1 to 2 weeks of pay per year | 2 to 4 weeks | 12 to 26 weeks |
| **Exempt / Professional Staff** | 2 to 3 weeks of pay per year | 4 to 8 weeks | 26 to 52 weeks |
| **Directors & Senior Management** | 3 to 4 weeks of pay per year | 12 to 26 weeks | 52 weeks |
| **C-Suite Executives** | Contractually defined golden parachutes | 6 to 12 months | 24 to 36 months |

## 2. Worked Severance Example

Consider a Senior Marketing Specialist with an annual salary of $78,000 ($1,500/week) who has served the company for **6 years and 4 months**:

* **Base Weekly Pay:** $\$78,000 \div 52 = \$1,500/\text{week}$
* **Company Policy:** 2 weeks of pay per completed year of service, with a 4-week minimum floor.
* **Calculation:** $6\text{ completed years} \times 2\text{ weeks} = \mathbf{12\text{ weeks of severance pay}}$
* **Gross Severance Payout:** $12 \times \$1,500 = \mathbf{\$18,000.00}$.

If the company also includes an accrued PTO payout (e.g., 60 hours unused vacation @ $37.50/hr = $2,250), the total separation package reaches **$20,250.00**.

## 3. How Is Severance Pay Taxed by the IRS?

A frequent shock to departing employees is the substantial tax withholding applied to severance checks.

Under the Internal Revenue Code, severance pay is categorized as **Supplemental Wages**. Employers usually withhold taxes using one of two methods:

### Method A: The Flat 22% Federal Supplemental Rate
For lump-sum payments up to $1 million, the IRS allows employers to withhold a flat **22% for federal income tax**, plus:
* **Social Security (FICA):** 6.2% (up to the annual statutory cap)
* **Medicare:** 1.45%
* **State Income Tax:** Varies by state (e.g., 6.6% in California, 4% to 8% in New York)
* **Combined Withholding:** Often totals **33% to 42%** of the gross lump-sum check!

### Method B: The Aggregate Method
The severance payout is combined with regular wages for the period, which may temporarily artificially inflate the employee's tax bracket, leading to higher withholding that is reconciled when filing an annual tax return.

## 4. Key Statutory Requirements: OWBPA & COBRA

1. **Older Workers Benefit Protection Act (OWBPA):** If an employee is **age 40 or older**, federal law gives them **21 days to consider** the severance agreement and a mandatory **7-day post-signing revocation period**. In group layoffs, a 45-day review period is required.
2. **COBRA Health Insurance:** Under federal COBRA, employers with 20+ employees must offer continuing health coverage for 18 months, though the employer may subsidize COBRA premiums as part of the severance package.

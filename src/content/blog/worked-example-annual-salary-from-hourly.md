Converting an hourly rate to annual salary sounds simple — multiply by 2,080 — but real-world calculations must account for overtime, paid time off, and benefits. This walkthrough uses the [salary converter](/calculators/salary-converter) to handle all three scenarios.

## The Scenario

Marcus earns **$25.00/hour** and is offered a salaried position. He wants to know what annual salary would be equivalent to his current hourly compensation. His current situation:

- **Base hours**: 40/week
- **Overtime**: Average 5 hours/week (paid at 1.5x)
- **Paid time off**: 2 weeks/year (80 hours)
- **Holidays**: 8 days/year (64 hours, paid)
- **Benefits**: Health insurance worth $8,000/year

Let's calculate three scenarios.

## Step 1: Calculate the Basic Annual Gross (2,080-Hour Rule)

The "quick math" annual salary calculation:

> Annual = Hourly rate × 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks)
> Annual = $25.00 × 2,080 = **$52,000 basic annual gross**

This is the number most people quote, but it's incomplete because it ignores:
- Overtime earnings
- Paid time off (PTO) value
- Holiday pay value
- Benefits value

## Step 2: Calculate Annual Gross WITH Overtime

Marcus averages 5 overtime hours per week at 1.5x:

> Regular pay: 40 hours × $25.00 = $1,000/week
> Overtime pay: 5 hours × $37.50 = $187.50/week
> Total weekly: $1,187.50

Annualized:

> $1,187.50 × 52 weeks = **$61,750 annual gross with overtime**

That's $9,750 MORE than the basic calculation — a 19% increase from 5 hours of weekly overtime.

## Step 3: Calculate PTO and Holiday Value

Marcus's paid time off has monetary value — he's paid for hours he doesn't work:

### Paid Time Off (2 weeks = 80 hours)

> PTO value: 80 hours × $25.00 = **$2,000**

### Paid Holidays (8 days = 64 hours)

> Holiday value: 64 hours × $25.00 = **$1,600**

### Total paid time off value

> $2,000 + $1,600 = **$3,600 in PTO/holiday pay**

## Step 4: Calculate Total Cash Compensation

Add base + OT + PTO:

> Basic annual gross: $52,000 (Step 1, includes PTO already)
> Overtime earnings: $9,750
> **Total cash compensation: $61,750**

Wait — the $52,000 in Step 1 already includes PTO (2,080 = 40 × 52, which assumes you're paid even during PTO weeks). So:

- If Marcus's 2,080 hours INCLUDE the 2 weeks of PTO (i.e., he's paid for PTO but doesn't work), then $52,000 includes PTO.
- If Marcus's 2,080 hours EXCLUDE PTO (i.e., he works 50 weeks + takes 2 weeks unpaid), then his actual worked hours are 2,000 and his gross is $50,000 + PTO $2,000 = $52,000.

Either way, the answer is the same. Let's clarify: Marcus works 40 hours × 50 weeks = 2,000 worked hours + takes 2 weeks paid PTO = 80 PTO hours.

So:

> Worked hours: 2,000 × $25.00 = $50,000
> Overtime: 5 hours × 50 weeks × $37.50 = $9,375
> PTO: 80 hours × $25.00 = $2,000
> Holiday pay: 64 hours × $25.00 = $1,600
> **Total cash compensation: $62,975**

## Step 5: Calculate Total Compensation Including Benefits

Add benefits value:

> Total cash: $62,975
> Plus health insurance: $8,000
> **Total compensation: $70,975**

## Step 6: Calculate Effective Hourly Rate (Total Comp)

If we divide total compensation by total hours worked (not PTO):

> $70,975 / 2,000 worked hours = **$35.49/hour effective rate**

Or by total paid hours (worked + PTO + holiday = 2,144):

> $70,975 / 2,144 paid hours = **$33.10/hour effective rate**

Either way, the effective hourly rate is much higher than the nominal $25.00/hour.

## Step 7: Calculate the Equivalent Annual Salary

If Marcus takes a salaried position with the same total compensation, the equivalent annual salary would be:

> **Equivalent annual salary: $70,975**

But this assumes the salaried position includes equivalent benefits. If the salaried role has:
- No overtime (but requires 45 hours/week) → Marcus effectively takes a pay cut
- Better benefits (401k match, better health insurance) → could be worth more
- Stock options or bonus → could exceed his current comp

## Step 9: Calculate Net Take-Home

After federal tax (12%), state tax (5%), and FICA (7.65%):

> Total tax: 24.65% effective rate
> Net take-home: $61,750 × (1 − 0.2465) = **$46,531 annual net**

For total compensation (including $8k health insurance, which is non-taxable):

> Cash net: $46,531
> Plus benefits value: $8,000 (non-taxable)
> **Effective total: $54,531/year**

## Common Conversion Mistakes

1. **Using 2,080 hours blindly** — this assumes no OT and no PTO; actual worked hours are often 1,900-2,000
2. **Ignoring overtime value** — 5 hours/week of OT adds $9,750/year at $25/hour
3. **Forgetting benefits** — health insurance alone can be $8,000-$15,000/year
4. **Not annualizing correctly** — bi-weekly is 26 paychecks (not 24, which is semi-monthly)
5. **Comparing gross only** — always compare total compensation, not just salary

## Step 11: Different Conversion Formulas

| Conversion | Formula |
|---|---|
| Hourly → Annual (no OT) | Hourly × 2,080 |
| Hourly → Annual (with OT) | (Reg hours × Hourly) + (OT hours × Hourly × 1.5) × 52 |
| Annual → Hourly | Annual / 2,080 |
| Monthly → Annual | Monthly × 12 |
| Bi-weekly → Annual | Bi-weekly × 26 |
| Weekly → Annual | Weekly × 52 |

Note: 2,080 hours assumes 40 hours × 52 weeks, INCLUDING paid time off. If you work 50 weeks (2 weeks unpaid), use 2,000 hours.

## Key Takeaways

- **2,080 hours is the standard but rarely the reality** — actual worked hours are 1,900-2,000
- **Overtime adds 15-20% to annual gross** for workers who average 5 hours/week OT
- **Benefits add 10-20% to total compensation** — health insurance alone is often $8k-$15k/year
- **Effective hourly rate** (total comp / worked hours) is the right metric for comparison
- **Always compare total compensation, not just salary** — benefits can swing the comparison by $10k+

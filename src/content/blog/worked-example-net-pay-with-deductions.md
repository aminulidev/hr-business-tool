Calculating take-home pay gets complicated once you add 401(k) contributions, health insurance, HSA, and especially a wage garnishment. This walkthrough shows every deduction step-by-step using the [payroll deduction calculator](/calculators/payroll-deduction-calculator).

## The Scenario

James earns **$72,000 annual salary** paid semi-monthly (24 paychecks per year). He contributes **6% to his 401(k)**, pays **$180/month for health insurance**, **$300/month for HSA**, and has a **15% wage garnishment** for unpaid taxes. Federal withholding is **14%** and state tax is **5%**. FICA applies at **7.65%**.

## Step 1: Calculate Gross Pay Per Paycheck

James is paid semi-monthly, so he gets 24 paychecks per year:

> $72,000 / 24 = **$3,000 gross per paycheck**

> $72,000 / 12 = $6,000 gross per month (for monthly deductions)

## Step 2: Calculate Pre-Tax Deductions

### 401(k) Contribution

401(k) is a **pre-tax deduction** — it reduces taxable income for federal and state tax (but NOT for FICA):

> $3,000 × 6% = **$180 per paycheck (401k)**

> Annual 401(k) contribution: $180 × 24 = **$4,320/year**

### Health Insurance Premium

Health insurance is also pre-tax for federal and state (but not FICA in most cases):

> $180/month ÷ 2 paychecks = **$90 per paycheck (health insurance)**

### HSA Contribution

HSA is pre-tax for federal, state, AND FICA:

> $300/month ÷ 2 paychecks = **$150 per paycheck (HSA)**

### Total Pre-Tax Deductions

> $180 (401k) + $90 (health) + $150 (HSA) = **$420 pre-tax deductions per paycheck**

## Step 3: Calculate Taxable Income

For federal and state tax, subtract 401(k), health, and HSA:

> $3,000 − $420 = **$2,580 taxable income (federal & state)**

For FICA, only HSA reduces the taxable base (401k and health insurance are still subject to FICA in most cases):

> $3,000 − $150 = **$2,850 taxable income (FICA)**

## Step 4: Calculate Each Tax

| Tax | Rate | Taxable Base | Tax Per Paycheck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal income tax | 14% | $2,580 | $361.20 |
| State income tax | 5% | $2,580 | $129.00 |
| FICA (Social Security + Medicare) | 7.65% | $2,850 | $217.96 |
| **Total taxes** | | | **$708.16** |

## Step 5: Calculate Post-Tax Garnishment

Wage garnishments for unpaid taxes are typically calculated as a percentage of **disposable income** (gross minus legally-required deductions like taxes and FICA). James's garnishment is 15% of disposable income:

> Disposable income = $3,000 − $708.16 (taxes) = $2,291.84
> Garnishment = $2,291.84 × 15% = **$343.78 per paycheck**

Note: Federal law (CCPA) caps garnishments at 25% of disposable income for most debts, so James's 15% is within legal limits.

## Step 6: Calculate Net Take-Home Pay

> Gross pay: $3,000.00
> Minus 401(k): −$180.00
> Minus health insurance: −$90.00
> Minus HSA: −$150.00
> Minus federal tax: −$361.20
> Minus state tax: −$129.00
> Minus FICA: −$217.96
> Minus garnishment: −$343.78
> **= $527.06 net take-home per paycheck**

Annual net take-home: $527.06 × 24 = **$12,649.44/year**

## Step 7: Understand Why Take-Home Is So Low

James's gross annual is $72,000 but his net is only $12,649 — that's a 82.4% reduction! Here's why:

1. **$4,320 goes to 401(k)** — invested for retirement, not lost
2. **$2,160 goes to health insurance** — necessary coverage
3. **$3,600 goes to HSA** — triple tax advantage, can be withdrawn
4. **$8,668.80 goes to federal tax** — at 14% effective rate
5. **$3,096 goes to state tax** — at 5% effective rate
6. **$5,231.04 goes to FICA** — Social Security + Medicare
7. **$8,250.72 goes to garnishment** — paying off tax debt

## Key Takeaways

- **Pre-tax deductions reduce taxes** — James saves $58.80/paycheck in federal tax alone from his 401(k) contribution
- **Garnishments are devastating** — 15% of disposable income is $343.78/paycheck or $8,250/year
- **HSA has triple tax advantage** — pre-tax for federal, state, AND FICA (unlike 401k)
- **Total deductions exceeded gross income share** — James keeps only 17.6% of his gross

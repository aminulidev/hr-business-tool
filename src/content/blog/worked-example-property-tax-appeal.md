Property tax is one of the largest fixed costs for homeowners and a complex calculation that varies by jurisdiction. This walkthrough uses the [property tax calculator](/calculators/property-tax-calculator) to compute the bill and walks through an appeal scenario.

## The Scenario

The Johnson family owns a home in Travis County, Texas (Austin area) with these characteristics:

- **Market value (assessed)**: $450,000
- **Assessment ratio**: 100% (Texas assesses at full market value)
- **Homestead exemption**: $25,000 (Texas standard homestead)
- **Local tax rates**:
  - City: 0.45%
  - County: 0.38%
  - School district: 1.12%
  - Special districts (hospital, community college): 0.15%
- **Total mill rate**: 2.10% (combined)

Let's calculate the annual property tax.

## Step 1: Calculate the Taxable Value

Texas assesses at 100% of market value, then subtracts exemptions:

> Market value: $450,000
> Minus homestead exemption: −$25,000
> **Taxable value: $425,000**

## Step 2: Calculate the Annual Property Tax

Apply the combined mill rate (2.10%):

> Annual property tax = Taxable value × Mill rate
> Annual property tax = $425,000 × 0.0210 = **$8,925/year**

## Step 3: Break Down by Taxing Authority

| Authority | Rate | Annual Tax |
|---|---|---|
| School district | 1.12% | $4,760.00 |
| City | 0.45% | $1,912.50 |
| County | 0.38% | $1,615.00 |
| Special districts | 0.15% | $637.50 |
| **Total** | **2.10%** | **$8,925.00** |

The school district typically collects 50-60% of total property tax.

## Step 4: Calculate Monthly Escrow Payment

Most mortgages include property tax in the monthly payment via an escrow account:

> Monthly escrow = $8,925 / 12 = **$743.75/month**

If the Johnsons' mortgage payment (principal + interest) is $2,200/month, the total monthly housing payment becomes:

> $2,200 + $743.75 (tax) + $150 (insurance) = **$3,093.75/month**

## Step 5: Calculate as % of Home Value

> Property tax as % of market value = $8,925 / $450,000 = **1.98%**

Texas has relatively high property taxes (no state income tax). National average is 1.1%; the Johnsons pay nearly double.

| Tax | Texas | California |

Texas is cheaper by $6,055/year for this income level — but the math flips at lower income levels.

## Step 7: Calculate 5-Year Projection

Property taxes typically rise 3-5%/year due to:

- Reassessment cycles (Texas reassesses annually)
- Voter-approved bond issues
- Rising home values

5-year projection at 4%/year growth:

| Year | Taxable Value | Annual Tax |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $425,000 | $8,925 |
| Year 2 | $442,000 | $9,282 |
| Year 3 | $459,680 | $9,653 |
| Year 4 | $478,067 | $10,039 |
| Year 5 | $497,190 | $10,441 |
| **5-year total** | — | **$48,340** |

In Texas, homestead exemptions cap annual assessed value increases at 10%/year, but at 4% growth, this cap doesn't bind.

## Step 8: Appeal Scenario

The Johnsons believe their $450,000 assessment is too high. Comparable sales in their neighborhood show similar homes selling for $400,000-$420,000. Let's calculate the potential savings from a successful appeal.

### If Assessed Value Reduced to $410,000

> New taxable value: $410,000 − $25,000 = $385,000
> New annual tax: $385,000 × 0.0210 = **$8,085/year**
> Annual savings: $8,925 − $8,085 = **$840/year**

### 5-Year Savings (Holding Reduction)

> Annual savings × 5 years (assuming value holds): $840 × 5 = **$4,200**

### Appeal Process Cost

- Filing fee: $15-$50 (varies by county)
- Appraisal report (if needed): $300-$500
- Time investment: 4-8 hours

> Total appeal cost: ~$400

> Net 5-year savings: $4,200 − $400 = **$3,800**
> ROI: ($4,200 − $400) / $400 = 950%

A successful property tax appeal has a 10x+ ROI for most homeowners.

## Step 10: How to Appeal Your Property Tax Assessment

### Step 1: Review Your Assessment Notice

Most jurisdictions send assessment notices in spring (March-May). Check the assessed value against:
- Recent comparable sales (within 6 months, within 0.5 miles)
- Your home's actual condition (any deferred maintenance?)
- Errors in property records (square footage, lot size, bedroom count)

### Step 2: Gather Evidence

Collect:
- 3-5 comparable sales at lower values than your assessment
- Photos of any property damage or deferred maintenance
- Recent appraisal (if you have one from a refinance)
- Property tax bills from neighbors with similar homes

### Step 3: File the Appeal

File with your county appraisal district (Texas) or assessor's office (other states). Deadlines are strict — typically 30-90 days after the assessment notice.

### Step 4: Attend the Hearing

Most appeals are heard by an Appraisal Review Board (ARB) or similar body. Present your evidence concisely (5-10 minutes). Bring 3 copies of all documents.

### Step 5: Negotiate if Possible

Many jurisdictions allow informal negotiation before the formal hearing. The ARB may offer a compromise reduction — assess whether it's worth accepting vs. the full appeal.

## Common Property Tax Mistakes

1. **Not checking the assessment annually** — assessment errors are common
2. **Missing the appeal deadline** — typically 30-90 days after notice
3. **Not claiming exemptions** — homestead, senior, veteran, disability exemptions vary by state
4. **Forgetting that property tax rises annually** — budget for 3-5% annual increases
5. **Not appealing when justified** — many homeowners leave money on the table

## Key Takeaways

- **Property tax is calculated on ASSESSED value, not market value** — assessment ratios vary by state (Texas = 100%, other states 10-40%)
- **Homestead exemptions reduce taxable value** — apply for all exemptions you qualify for
- **Property tax rises 3-5%/year** — budget for annual increases
- **Appeals have 10x+ ROI** when the assessment is genuinely too high
- **School district typically collects 50-60% of total property tax** — local school bonds directly affect your bill

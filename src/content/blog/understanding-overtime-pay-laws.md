Overtime compensation is a frequent source of confusion and potential legal risk for businesses. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), most non-exempt employees must be paid overtime for hours worked over 40 in a workweek.

## The Time-and-a-Half Rule

The standard overtime rate is at least 1.5 times the employee's regular rate of pay. This means if an employee earns $20 per hour, their overtime rate should be at least $30 per hour.

## Exempt vs. Non-Exempt Employees

Not all employees are eligible for overtime. "Exempt" employees are typically salaried and fall into categories like executive, administrative, professional, or outside sales roles. However, meeting the salary threshold alone isn't enough; they must also meet specific "duties tests."

## Calculating Overtime for Different Pay Structures

Calculating overtime becomes more complex when employees receive bonuses, commissions, or have multiple pay rates. All non-discretionary bonuses must be included in the "regular rate of pay" when calculating overtime.

## State-Specific Overtime Laws

Some states have stricter overtime rules than the federal government. For example, California requires daily overtime pay for hours worked beyond eight in a single day, regardless of the weekly total.
